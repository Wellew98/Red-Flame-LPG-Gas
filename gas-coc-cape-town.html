When you need a COC, and who can issue one.

The most common trigger in Cape Town is a property sale: a change of ownership legally requires a valid gas COC if the property has an LPG installation, and conveyancers across the City Bowl, Atlantic Seaboard and Southern Suburbs routinely hold up transfers over a missing or expired one. A COC is also required on every new gas installation, and whenever an existing one is altered, an appliance added, a cylinder bank resized, or piping moved during a renovation.

Only an installer currently registered with SAQCC Gas may issue a valid certificate. One signed by someone without current registration isn't a legal document, whatever it looks like on paper, and will be rejected by insurers, conveyancers and the authorities. The check that catches renovations out.

No plug points within 200mm of a burner or gas release point

SANS 10087 requires at least 200mm of clearance between any plug point or light switch and a gas burner or release point, and no plug points at all in the same partition as an internal cylinder. It's one of the most common reasons an installation doesn't pass first time: an electrician places a socket exactly where the gas point was meant to go, often mid-renovation, and the certificate can't be signed until it moves.

Worth checking before the tiling or the cabinetry goes in, not after. If you're planning a renovation, send us the layout and we'll tell you whether the gas point and the sockets can live where they're drawn.

Our inspection checklist.

Nine checks on every gas COC inspection, whether it is for a property sale, a new installation or an insurance requirement.

  1. Visual assessment of pipework and connections
  2. Leak detection on every joint
  3. Pressure test of the full system
  4. Valve function and shut-off check
  5. Regulator condition and corrosion check
  6. Safety distances between cylinders, appliances and electrics
  7. Pipe condition, including hose date codes
  8. Ventilation and airflow around cylinders and appliances
  9. Final documentation and certificate issue

What a gas COC does not cover.

Worth knowing before you book, so there are no surprises at the door.

  • Portable appliances not permanently connected, braais, patio heaters and camping stoves
  • The appliance itself, the COC certifies the installation, not whether the hob or geyser unit is in good working order
  • Cylinder condition, cylinders are inspected separately by the gas supplier
  • Ongoing appliance servicing, a COC is not a maintenance contract
  • Any change made to the installation after the inspection date, a new appliance or altered piping needs a new COC

Gas COC questions Cape Town customers ask.

How much does a gas certificate of compliance cost?
From R950 for the first property or appliance, R150 for each additional appliance inspected on the same visit. If the installation fails and needs repair before we can certify it, remedial work is quoted on site. An inspection report only, without a repair, is R750 including the callout and labour.
How do I get a gas certificate of compliance?
Book a SAQCC registered installer to inspect the installation. We check pipework, joints, valves, regulators and safety distances, pressure test the system, fix any faults found, and issue the Certificate of Compliance once everything passes.
How long is a gas compliance certificate valid?
A gas COC has no fixed statutory expiry, but it is only valid for the installation as it existed on the inspection date. Any change to the appliances or piping after that date requires a new COC. For a property sale, most conveyancers require a COC issued within the last 3 months.
Do I need a gas COC to sell my house?
Yes. A valid gas Certificate of Compliance is a legal requirement for a property transfer in South Africa if the property has an LPG gas installation. It should be no older than 3 months at the date of sale.
Do I need a COC for a 9kg gas bottle?
If the 9kg cylinder is permanently connected to an appliance such as a hob, stove or geyser, yes, that installation needs a COC. A 9kg bottle used loose on a portable braai or camping stove does not need one.
What happens if my installation fails the inspection?
We tell you exactly what failed and quote the repair on site. Because Red Flame is a registered installer, not just an inspector, we fix the fault and certify the installation on the same visit in most cases, instead of referring the repair to a separate contractor.
