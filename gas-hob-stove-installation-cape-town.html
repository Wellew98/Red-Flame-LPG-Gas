What a hob or stove installation involves.

Running the gas line from the cylinder to the appliance, fitting the regulator and isolation valve, checking clearances around the burners, pressure testing the connection for leaks, and signing the Gas Certificate of Compliance once it passes. A single hob connection in a Woodstock or City Bowl flat is usually the simplest install we do. A freestanding stove, or a kitchen renovation with a moved gas point, takes longer.

Internal or external cylinder setup.

Where the cylinder sits decides most of the job. We assess the kitchen on site and recommend whichever setup actually suits how it gets used.

Cylinder inside the cupboard

A ventilated kitchen cupboard holding a 9kg cylinder is the standard setup for a single hob in a small flat or apartment, common across Woodstock and the City Bowl. No plug points may sit in the same partition as the cylinder. It stays out of sight and the cupboard door is the only access anyone needs.

Cylinder outside in a cage

A freestanding stove, a double hob, or a kitchen that also feeds a braai point usually needs an external cylinder or bank instead, mounted in a gas cage outside. Bigger cylinders mean longer between swaps, and nobody has to enter the house to change one.

The 200mm rule.

No plug points within 200mm of a burner or gas release point

SANS 10087 requires at least 200mm of clearance between any plug point or light switch and a gas burner or release point, and no plug points at all in the same partition as an internal cylinder. This trips up a lot of DIY kitchen renovations. An electrician places a socket exactly where the hob was meant to go, and the installation fails compliance before it starts.

Worth checking before the tiling or the cabinetry goes in, not after. If you are mid-renovation, send us the kitchen plan and we will tell you whether the gas point and the sockets can live where they are drawn.

What moves the price.

  • Distance from cylinder to hob. Every extra metre of gas line adds material and labour.
  • Whether the gas point moves. Connecting where the point already sits is the cheapest version of this job.
  • Internal cupboard or external cage. An external setup means a wall penetration and usually a cage.
  • Drop-in hob or freestanding stove. A freestanding stove needs a flexible connection and more clearance checks.
  • Existing pipework. If there is old pipe or an unsafe previous install, it gets quoted separately.
  • Access. A ground-floor Woodstock flat is quicker than a third-floor unit with no lift.

Hob and stove questions Cape Town customers ask.

How much does a gas hob installation cost?
From R2,500 for a standard installation, including the COC. That is a starting price, quoted in full after an on-site assessment. You buy the hob or stove from your own supplier. We supply the cylinders and the cage where one is required. WhatsApp your kitchen layout for an exact quote.
Can a 9kg cylinder go inside a kitchen cupboard?
Yes, provided the cupboard is properly ventilated and no plug points or light switches sit in the same partition as the cylinder. This is a standard setup for a single hob in a small Cape Town kitchen.
Do I need a COC for a gas hob?
Yes. Any permanently connected gas hob or stove installation requires a Gas Certificate of Compliance under SANS 10087. We issue it on the same visit once the installation passes the pressure test.
Can you connect a stove I already bought?
Yes. You buy the hob or stove from your own supplier, and we install it. We install any brand, and the installation, gas line and COC are the same job either way. We do supply the cylinders and the cage where one is needed.
How long does a hob installation take?
Usually 1 to 2 hours for a single hob or stove connection, including the pressure test and the COC paperwork.
